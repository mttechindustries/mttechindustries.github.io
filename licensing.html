Commercialization pathways

Start with the smallest engagement that can produce external evidence.

MT Tech is in early commercialization. The appropriate entry point depends on what must be proven: buyer value, software workflow, physical validation, or strategic IP fit. Detailed technical material remains controlled.

Revenue / customer-validation lane

Cognitive Dynamics — bounded assessment first.

The current lead commercial hypothesis is a defined AI Integrity Assessment rather than an assumed full SaaS sale. A first engagement can establish target scope, assessment workflow, report usefulness, remediation value, delivery effort, and willingness to continue paying.

Paid assessment

Agreed system and scope, structured evidence, findings, report, and remediation discussion.

Design-partner pilot

A bounded early engagement with shared KPIs and explicit product-learning goals.

Repeat / platform path

Recurring assessment, API/software access, or licensing only after the first workflow is externally validated.

R&D / sensing-validation lane

WoofWizeX-FRS — research and validation partnership.

This lane requires evidence that software packaging alone cannot create: representative data, physical capture provenance, field performance, false-positive characterization, and prospective domain validation. The appropriate conversation is therefore a research, design-partner, sponsored-development, or non-dilutive validation engagement.

Alternate B2B route

Seer² — controlled signal-integrity evaluation.

A qualified organization with a defined security, privacy, facility, or research problem may create a higher-value evaluation opportunity. The public position remains bounded to implemented software and validated physical paths; unvalidated sensor modes are not marketed as finished capabilities.

Filed IP

Selective licensing follows verified scope and diligence.

MT Tech has five U.S. patent filings: one non-provisional application and four provisional applications. A filing is not an issued patent and is not a market-readiness claim. Field-of-use, joint-development, or strategic licensing conversations require a defined asset, disclosure boundary, and written agreement.
